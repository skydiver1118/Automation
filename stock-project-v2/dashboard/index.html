Method, data coverage & limitations

How ideas are selected

Best supported: stocks with long-term score ≥55, quality ≥55, positive free-cash-flow yield and operating margin, and at least 80% observed inputs. Value + quality also requires quality and value scores ≥60. These are transparent screening rules, not backtested return forecasts.

“In entry zone” also requires price within the calculated support band, entry score ≥65, positive MACD, price above 50DMA, and RSI below 70. Stop references are technical levels, not guaranteed execution prices.

Comparable changes only

Daily and weekly changes use the exact previous NYSE session and five sessions earlier. Method version, scoring fingerprint and the complete ticker set must match. A missing session is not skipped. Positive rank change means moving toward rank 1; tied scores share rank.

History breaks at method or universe resets. Older snapshots without verified methodology are isolated points. The initial August 14 point is hidden, with original records preserved.

Reading scores and coverage

Scores compare this selected universe on a 0–100 scale. They are not return estimates or probabilities. Stocks and ETFs retain different composite formulas; use the asset filter when comparing like investments.

Coverage counts observed raw inputs: 23 for stocks and 11 for ETFs. It does not certify source accuracy or freshness. Missing factors are neutralized at 50 by the existing engine, so a score may look stronger than its evidence.

Diagnostic bullish percentages measure the share of directional indicators, not the probability of a price increase.

Sources & research gaps

Fundamentals and estimates are retrieved at refresh time; they are not reconstructed historical vintages. Scoring weights have not been validated here as predictors of future returns. Secular durability is currently neutral rather than independently researched.

Sector filters help reveal clusters; owning more tickers in the same theme does not ensure diversification. See FINRA on concentration risk and SEC guidance on performance claims.
